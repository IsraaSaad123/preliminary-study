# Data Protection Provisions (GDPR)

These provisions are drawn from the General Data Protection Regulation. They reflect
both the EU GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) and the UK GDPR, which are materially
equivalent for the provisions listed here. Each provision is assigned a stable code
(OB1, OB2, ...). When deriving regulatory obligations for a feature, refer to these
provisions by their codes.

---

**OB1: Lawfulness of processing (Art. 6).**
Processing is lawful only if at least one legal basis applies, for example the data
subject's consent, performance of a contract, or the legitimate interests of the
controller. The applicable legal basis must be identified for each processing activity.

**OB2: Consent (Art. 7).**
Where processing relies on consent, the controller must be able to demonstrate that the
data subject consented. Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and
unambiguous, and it must be as easy to withdraw as to give.

**OB3: Conditions applicable to a child's consent in relation to information society services (Art. 8).**
Where information society services are offered directly to a child and processing is
based on consent, that consent is lawful only where the child is at least 16 years old
(Member States may lower this to not below 13). Where the child is below that age,
consent must be given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility, and the
controller must make reasonable efforts to verify such authorisation.

**OB4: Processing of special categories of personal data (Art. 9).**
Processing of special categories of data (for example, data concerning health, or
biometric data processed to uniquely identify a person) is prohibited unless a specific
exception applies, such as the data subject's explicit consent.

**OB5: Principles: purpose limitation (Art. 5(1)(b)).**
Personal data must be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and
not further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes. The purpose of
processing must be defined.

**OB6: Principles: data minimisation (Art. 5(1)(c)).**
Personal data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation
to the purposes for which they are processed.

**OB7: Principles: storage limitation (Art. 5(1)(e)).**
Personal data must be kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for
no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the data are processed. A
retention period (or the criteria used to determine it) must be defined.

**OB8: Information to be provided where data are collected from the data subject (Art. 13).**
Where personal data are collected, the controller must provide the data subject with
specified information at the time of collection, including the purposes of processing,
the legal basis, the recipients, and the retention period.

**OB9: Right to erasure (Art. 17).**
The data subject has the right to obtain erasure of personal data without undue delay in
certain circumstances, for example where the data are no longer necessary for the
purposes, or where consent is withdrawn and there is no other legal basis.

**OB10: Security of processing (Art. 32).**
The controller must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to
ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, taking into account the nature,
scope, and purposes of processing.

---

When producing the obligation specification, identify which of the above provisions
(OB1–OB10) apply to the feature, and express each resulting obligation with reference
to its provision code.

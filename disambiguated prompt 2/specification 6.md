# Regulatory Obligation Specification — Maths Numeracy Voice Practice Feature

## Feature Summary

Students practice basic maths numeracy by speaking answers aloud. Voice audio is captured
via the device microphone and processed by a speech-recognition component solely to
transcribe the spoken numerical content of each answer; it is not used to identify or
distinguish individual speakers. Transcribed answers are evaluated and immediate feedback
is given. All student responses are logged in an in-app history so that students can
review previous attempts and replay lessons.

## Applicable Provisions and Obligations

OB1: The controller must identify and document a lawful basis under Art. 6 for each
processing activity: (a) capturing and processing voice audio for speech recognition, and
(b) logging and retaining student response history to support lesson review and replay.

OB2: Where consent is the chosen legal basis, the controller must be able to demonstrate
that consent was freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous; the controller must
provide a mechanism to withdraw consent that is as easy to use as the mechanism to give
it, and must cease the relevant processing upon withdrawal.

OB3: Because the service is offered directly to students who may be children, where
processing relies on consent the controller must verify that each student meets the
applicable minimum age (at least 13 years under a Member State derogation, or 16 years
otherwise); for students below that age, consent must be given or authorised by the
holder of parental responsibility, and the controller must make reasonable efforts to
verify that such authorisation has been provided.

OB5: The purposes of processing must be explicitly defined — voice audio is processed
exclusively to recognise the spoken numerical content of answers, and response logs are
retained exclusively to enable student review of previous attempts and lesson replay;
neither the audio nor the response logs may be processed in any manner incompatible with
those stated purposes.

OB6: Only the data strictly necessary for the defined purposes may be collected and
retained; raw voice audio must not be stored beyond the point at which the spoken answer
has been successfully transcribed (unless audio retention is itself required for the
replay functionality), and response logs must contain no more information than is
necessary to support review and replay.

OB7: Student response logs (and any retained audio segments) must be kept for no longer
than is necessary for lesson-review and replay purposes; a retention period must be
defined and enforced, and data must be deleted or anonymised once that period expires.
[retention: 12 months from the date of collection]

OB8: At the point of collecting voice audio and response data, the controller must inform
the student — and, where the student is a child below the applicable age threshold, the
holder of parental responsibility — of the purposes of processing, the legal basis, any
recipients of the data, and the retention period that applies to response logs and any
retained audio. [retention: 12 months from the date of collection]

OB9: The controller must provide a mechanism for students (or, for children, their parent
or guardian) to request erasure of their response logs and any retained audio data, and
must action such requests without undue delay where no other legal basis for continued
retention applies.

OB10: The controller must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to
secure voice audio in transit, transcribed responses, and stored response logs, taking
into account the heightened risk associated with processing personal data relating to
children; measures must include, at minimum, encryption of data in transit and at rest
and strict access controls limiting who can read stored response logs.

## Provision Codes That Apply

OB1, OB2, OB3, OB5, OB6, OB7, OB8, OB9, OB10

## Provision Codes That Do Not Apply

OB4: Voice audio is processed solely to recognise the spoken numerical content of
answers and is not processed to uniquely identify or distinguish individual speakers;
it therefore does not constitute biometric data processed for identification purposes
within the meaning of Art. 9, and no other special-category data is involved in this
feature.

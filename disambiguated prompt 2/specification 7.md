# Regulatory Obligation Specification — Maths Numeracy Voice Practice Feature

## Feature Summary

Students practice basic maths using their device microphone. Speech recognition transcribes spoken numerical answers and provides immediate feedback. Voice audio is processed solely to recognise the spoken numerical content; it is not used to identify or distinguish individual speakers. Student responses are logged in app history so students can review previous attempts and replay lessons.

## Applicable Provisions and Obligations

OB1: The controller must identify and document a valid legal basis (e.g., consent, legitimate interests, or contractual necessity) for each processing activity — specifically for microphone audio capture during answer recognition and for the persistent logging of student responses in app history. [retention: n/a]

OB2: Where consent is the chosen legal basis, the controller must obtain freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous consent from the data subject before processing begins, and must provide a mechanism to withdraw consent that is as easy to use as the mechanism to grant it. [retention: n/a]

OB3: Because the service is offered directly to students who may be under the applicable age threshold (16, or as low as 13 where a Member State has exercised that option), where processing relies on consent the controller must make reasonable efforts to verify that consent for any student below that age has been given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility before any audio capture or response logging begins. [retention: n/a]

OB5: The controller must define the purposes of processing in specific and explicit terms — namely, real-time speech-to-text conversion for answer recognition and persistent logging for student self-review — and must not use the audio data or response logs for any purpose incompatible with those defined purposes (e.g., speaker profiling, behavioural analytics, or third-party advertising). [retention: n/a]

OB6: The controller must limit the data collected to what is strictly necessary for the defined purposes: microphone audio should be processed transiently for numerical recognition and not retained in raw audio form beyond the moment of transcription; only the transcribed answer, the associated question, the timestamp, and the outcome need be stored in the response log. [retention: n/a]

OB7: Student response logs must be retained for no longer than is necessary for the purpose of self-review and lesson replay. The controller must define and enforce a retention period of 12 months from the date each response is recorded, after which response log entries must be deleted or anonymised; logs must also be deleted promptly upon account closure or withdrawal of consent, whichever occurs first. [retention: 12 months from the date of each recorded response, or upon account closure, whichever is sooner]

OB8: At the point of first collection — before microphone access is granted and before any response is logged — the controller must provide each student (and, where OB3 applies, the holder of parental responsibility) with the following information: the identity of the controller, the purposes of processing (answer recognition and response history), the legal basis relied upon, the retention period (12 months or until account closure), any recipients of the data, and the existence of rights including the right to erasure. [retention: 12 months from the date of each recorded response, or upon account closure, whichever is sooner]

OB9: The controller must provide students (and parental responsibility holders where applicable) with a mechanism to request erasure of their response log entries, and must fulfil such requests without undue delay where the data are no longer necessary for the purpose, or where consent has been withdrawn and no other legal basis applies. [retention: n/a]

OB10: The controller must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to secure both the transient audio stream during processing and the persisted response log data, including measures appropriate to the risk posed by processing data relating to children, such as encryption in transit and at rest, access controls limiting who can read response histories, and regular review of those controls. [retention: n/a]

## Provisions Not Applicable

**OB4** (special categories — biometric data): The feature explicitly does not process voice audio to uniquely identify or distinguish individual speakers; the audio is used solely for numerical content recognition. Because the biometric data definition under Art. 9 requires processing aimed at unique identification of a person, OB4 does not apply to this feature as described.
